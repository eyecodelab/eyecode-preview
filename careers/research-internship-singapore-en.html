How to Find a Research Internship in Singapore

Published Nov 8, 2026

Find suitable research groups, prepare a credible application, and discuss supervision while checking funding and immigration arrangements separately.

Singapore

Research Intern · Research Engineer · applications · academic-cv · research-internship

Kinh nghiệm tìm Research Internship tại Singapore

Start with a research question, not a mailing list

Finding a research internship should not begin with sending the same CV to every professor in Singapore. A useful application explains which problems interest you, what preparation you can demonstrate, and when you could participate. Your immediate goal is not to prove that you know all of AI. It is to give a research group enough relevant information for a meaningful conversation.

This is editorial preparation advice informed by official sources, not an applicant’s personal account or a recruitment announcement. Named programs and laboratories are information sources, not confirmation of an available vacancy. EyeCode is not recruiting research interns through this article.

Find programs and laboratories through official pages

Starting points include NTU’s Global Connect Fellowship [1], the NUS Smart Systems Institute [2], and the undergraduate internship information at NUS Acoustic Research Laboratory [3]. They illustrate different information structures: a university program, an interdisciplinary institute, and a specialist laboratory. Do not assume their eligibility rules or application procedures are interchangeable.

For each relevant group, record its research area, recent work, potential supervisor, official application route, and the date you checked the information. Mark unanswered questions explicitly. A laboratory description is not evidence of an internship opening. Look for contact instructions or ask a coordinator rather than inferring deadlines from an old social-media post.

Separate research fit from participation requirements

Check academic stage, subject background, enrollment status, availability, home-university requirements, and the working arrangement before investing heavily in an application. Then assess research fit: can you explain the group’s questions, which skills are relevant, and where would you need guidance? Academic suitability does not automatically establish administrative eligibility.

Eligibility, compensation, and immigration or work arrangements depend on the institution, program, and applicant. Singapore’s Ministry of Manpower publishes specific rules on work-pass exemptions for foreign students [4]; an international internship does not automatically qualify. Ask the host to confirm the applicable process, responsible contact, and required timing. This article does not determine which permission applies to your circumstances.

Make your academic CV and GitHub evidence useful

An academic CV should show education, relevant research or coursework projects, evidenced skills, and referees who have agreed to be contacted. Explain the problem, your contribution, evaluation method, and actual outputs for each project. If you have no publication, describe a report or reproduction study accurately. Do not label an unreviewed manuscript as an accepted paper.

Choose a repository whose purpose, setup, data origin, and limitations can be understood quickly. GitHub describes the README’s role in helping others understand and start using a project [5]. Add your own assumptions, baseline, and unresolved errors. Remove private data, access keys, and materials you cannot share. A small, responsible repository is stronger evidence than a collection of inaccessible links.

Contact a supervisor with a specific reason

Read the laboratory page and a relevant research contribution well enough to summarize the central question in your own words. You do not need to promise an immediate breakthrough. Explain what interests you, which experience connects to it, and what you hope to investigate. Where a program requires a central application portal, follow that process; email is not a way to bypass it.

A suggested structure—not a real email or a claimed successful application—is a short introduction and availability, one specific research connection, evidence from a personal project, a question about the appropriate application route, and links to a CV and selected repository. Rewrite it for the situation. Avoid mass messages, generic praise, large unsolicited attachments, and interpreting silence as a verdict on your ability.

Prepare a technical discussion and questions of your own

Practice explaining a project from problem to conclusion. The listener should understand where the data came from, why the baseline was appropriate, which errors you investigated, and what remains uncertain. If you do not know an answer, state an assumption and suggest how to test it. The discussion concerns learning and collaboration as well as technical preparation.

  • Ask who would supervise your day-to-day work and how communication is organized.
  • Clarify project scope, access to data, and computing resources.
  • Discuss expected outputs, contribution attribution, and rules for sharing code or publishing.
  • Confirm availability, financial arrangements, accommodation if offered, and administrative support.

Discuss authorship and contribution expectations early without treating publication as a guaranteed benefit. Scope may change and experiments may not support a hypothesis. Agreement on working practices, reporting standards, and what you may later show in a portfolio is more useful than an informal promise of a paper.

Avoid preventable mistakes and maintain a tracking process

Check recipient names, the fit between your CV and the research area, repository links, and the accuracy of contribution statements. Do not discuss funding while showing no understanding of the project; equally, do not ignore costs and paperwork because the institution is well known. Both intellectual fit and practical feasibility deserve attention.

Track each application separately: official source, checked requirements, CV version, contact date, and next step. Follow up only when appropriate under the institution’s instructions. Recheck program pages before committing because application periods and policies can change. Preparation cannot guarantee selection, but it can help you choose relevant opportunities and communicate with care.
