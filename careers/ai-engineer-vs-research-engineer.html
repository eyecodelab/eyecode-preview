AI Engineer vs. Research Engineer: What's the Difference?

Published Nov 8, 2026

Compare responsibilities, deliverables, and skills through a practical example, then choose a direction using evidence from your own work.

Vietnam · Singapore

AI Engineer · Research Engineer · career-stories · research · production

AI Engineer và Research Engineer khác nhau thế nào?

Start with deliverables, not the job title

One AI Engineer vacancy may focus on deploying models; another may emphasize experiments. A Research Engineer does not simply spend the day reading papers. Choosing a path from the title alone can leave a student with an impressive-looking portfolio that solves none of the team’s actual problems. A better starting point is to ask what the person must deliver, who uses that work, and how the team decides whether it is reliable.

This article compares two working priorities rather than ranking professions or describing every employer. The scenarios are explicitly hypothetical editorial examples, not interviews or personal experiences. For a position in Vietnam or Singapore, check every assumption against the actual job description and a conversation with the hiring team.

Two priorities you may encounter

In this comparison, a product-oriented AI Engineer makes an AI capability useful inside a working process: inputs arrive in the right format, predictions are served reliably, and failures have an owner. A research-oriented Research Engineer makes a hypothesis testable: implementing methods, constructing meaningful comparisons, and investigating results that cannot be reproduced. Both need strong software skills. The distinction is the question their work is trying to answer.

Google DeepMind describes Research Engineers as a bridge between ideas and implementation [1]. Google Research also describes engineers who build large-scale experiments [2]. These are organizational examples, not universal degree requirements or hiring standards.

A comparison framework for reading vacancies

An editorial framework; actual responsibilities may overlap
DimensionProduct-oriented AI EngineerResearch-oriented Research Engineer
Central questionIs the solution useful and operationally reliable?Does the idea work under controlled conditions?
DeliverablesPipelines, inference services, tests, operational documentationExperiment code, configurations, baselines, analysis
EvaluationPrediction quality alongside latency, cost, and failure handlingCorrectness, reproducibility, and limits of the conclusion
CollaboratorsProduct, backend, data, operations, and usersResearchers, infrastructure engineers, and data owners

Use the table to ask questions, not to create a rigid boundary. A small team may need one person to cover both priorities. A larger team may divide ownership into narrow components. Ask which responsibilities belong to the role and which belong elsewhere.

A hypothetical visual inspection project

Imagine a team using photographs to support product inspection. On the product side, you would clarify which defects need reporting, where images originate, what happens when a camera fails, and who confirms uncertain predictions. A strong notebook score does not settle those questions. A handover might include an API, input validation tests, setup instructions, and a way to restore an earlier version after a problem.

On the research side, the question might be whether a training method improves robustness to changing illumination. You would preserve comparable data splits, choose appropriate baselines, and inspect the image groups that fail. A negative finding can be valuable if it convincingly rules out a hypothesis. No numerical result is invented for this example: its purpose is to show how responsibilities lead to different evidence.

What the work asks you to practice

For product work, practice following an issue through reproduction, logs, diagnosis, tests, and an explanation of impact. Google’s Rules of Machine Learning is a useful reference for thinking beyond the algorithm [3]. In a portfolio, a modest model with an understandable operating process can demonstrate this habit better than an elaborate model with no failure analysis.

For research work, rewrite a method’s assumptions in your own words before implementing it. Keep records of configurations, data versions, and reasons for changes. The scikit-learn pitfalls guide is a reference for checking preprocessing and data leakage [4]. The professional habit is to distinguish a justified experiment from repeated parameter changes until an attractive number appears.

Build evidence that matches the work

  • Product direction: a repository with setup commands, tests, input contracts, and documented limitations.
  • Research direction: a reproduction report with a clear baseline and an explanation of deviations from the original method.
  • Both directions: precise attribution of your contribution to a group project.

In an interview, explain one decision in depth: why a metric was appropriate, which data you excluded, or how you discovered an error. A long list of libraries is less informative. Call an undeployed system a prototype. If reproduction is incomplete, show the mismatch and what you checked rather than describing it as a successful result.

Choose your next step through a small experiment

You do not need to select an entire career while still studying. Produce two outputs from one project: a demonstrable handover and a controlled experiment report. Notice which part makes you curious, which part you handle carefully, and what feedback is still missing. Ask an experienced practitioner to review both outputs rather than asking only which title sounds better.

Early on, focus on completing a bounded task with guidance and reporting where you need help. With experience, aim to own a component, help others review results, and coordinate decisions across a team. These responsibilities may support progression to senior or technical leadership roles, depending on the organization; they are not a mandatory title ladder.

Progression may mean owning a component, a production system, or a research direction; there is no universal promotion timetable. When comparing opportunities in either market, ask about mentoring, data access, and evaluation practices. Choose an environment that helps you produce credible work, then let that evidence guide the next decision.
